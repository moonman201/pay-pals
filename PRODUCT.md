# Pay Pals

**Two AI agents. One safe deal.**

Pay Pals puts an AI agent on each side of a stranger deal. The buyer's pal vets the listing, the seller's pal locks the item into a PayPal Goods & Services payment, and both keep proof.

## The problem
Every day, people buy used gear, sneakers, cards, tickets and electronics from strangers on online marketplaces and resale groups. To dodge the Goods & Services fee, buyers get talked into paying with Friends & Family. Friends & Family has no protection. That one choice is where most of these scams happen, and it is also money PayPal never earns.

## What Pay Pals does
- **Seller Pal** (gold) turns a listing into an item card: model, serial number read from the photos, condition, what is included, price. When both sides agree, it locks that card into a PayPal checkout order paid to the seller. After payment it walks the seller through packing photos and tracking.
- **Buyer Pal** (blue) reviews the deal before money moves: checks the price against real market prices, reads the chat for scam patterns (Friends & Family requests, pressure, off-platform payment, mismatched names, stock photos), asks for what is missing, and gives a plain verdict. After delivery it checks what arrived against the locked card.
- **The deal itself** is a PayPal Goods & Services checkout payment. The buyer pays with the PayPal button or Venmo. The exact item description lives inside PayPal's own record of the payment, so both sides are covered on the same terms.
- **If something goes wrong,** either pal builds an evidence pack: the locked card, photos, chat, tracking, timeline. The buyer opens a normal PayPal dispute with it.

## Why PayPal cares
- PayPal earns on Goods & Services and earns nothing on Friends & Family. Pay Pals makes the protected choice the easy default.
- Fewer scams, fewer messy disputes, clearer evidence when one happens.
- It is agentic commerce people already need: two agents representing two humans, settling through PayPal.

## Who it is for
Anyone buying or selling with a stranger: resale groups, collectors, people selling gear before a move.
